# Capstone Project Ideas

Pick one question (alone or in a small team) and: (1) state a hypothesis that you think best answers it; (2) develop a model capable of testing the hypothesis; (3) test the hypothesis with model runs and any additional analysis; and (4) present your work.

**1. A mobile bed in the Tar River.** The [Tar River exercise](../../../03-ode-applications/exercises/tar-river-and-plume/index.md) assumed a static bed and no sediment transport. What if the bed were mobile and the river carried sediment from upstream?

1. What mass or volume of sediment deposits or erodes in each of the two conditions over three days? Where does it occur, and which condition differs most from the normal case?
2. For each condition, what would the bed and water surface profiles look like if the bed reached a steady state with the boundary conditions?

**2. Limits of the integral plume model.** The plume model treats velocity as a function of cross-shore distance only and neglects Coriolis. How reasonable are these assumptions? Where are they more valid? How do Coriolis and added dimensions change the results?

**3. 1D versus 2D in HEC-RAS.** Set up 1D and 2D HEC-RAS projects of the same region and compare a 1D, a 2D diffusion-wave and a 2D full-momentum simulation. When does it make sense to move to each next level of complexity? *(Requires the HEC-RAS modules, which will follow.)*

**4. Salt wedges.** What governs whether a salt wedge forms in a laterally confined estuary (think the lower Mississippi or the Brazos River)? Which parameters control its location? What is needed to expel all the saltwater?

**5. A dam on a gravel-bed river.** How will a gravel-bed river (a mix of sand, gravel and cobbles) at steady state adjust to a new dam? Will the bed erode or aggrade downstream, and what happens to the downstream grain-size distribution? What happens if the dam is later removed, introducing additional sand to the reach?
